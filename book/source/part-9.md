# PART IX — 3:00 AM AGAIN

## 54. Another Tuesday

Months later, on a Tuesday that resembles the one this account opened with closely enough that describing it separately feels almost redundant, the badge comes off at the usual hour, the M.Tech lecture recording gets watched at 1.5x, and by nine p.m. there is, once again, officially nothing scheduled.

Some things have changed in the interval, in ways too small to have registered as change while they were happening. The TKI agent now reaches for four tools instead of one, reliably enough that he's stopped watching every single run the way he watched the first ones, the way you stop checking on a plant once it's demonstrated it can survive a week without you hovering over it. The portfolio hasn't been rebuilt from scratch again, which may itself be a kind of milestone, though he wouldn't have predicted, a year ago, that "didn't rewrite it entirely" would ever register as an accomplishment. The reconciliation tooling at work has a second efficiency win attached to it now, smaller than the first, unremarked upon by anyone but him. The stranger who fixed the tool registration bug opened a second pull request three weeks ago, this one adding a small feature rather than fixing anything broken, which he took, privately, as a good sign — a contributor doesn't add features to a project they've already decided to abandon.

None of this adds up to a transformation. He is not meaningfully a different person than he was on the Tuesday this account started with, and he'd be suspicious of any account of the last several months that tried to claim otherwise. What's changed is smaller and more honest than a transformation: a slightly larger set of things he understands from the inside instead of only from the outside, a slightly shorter list of assumptions he hasn't yet tested, a tab graveyard that's been culled and has already, predictably, reconstituted itself past thirty.

He checks GitHub notifications, the same reflex as always. Someone starred a repository. A dependency needs updating somewhere he's half-forgotten the purpose of. Nothing urgent.

And then, because this is apparently just how it goes now, because the mechanism never really changed even as everything downstream of it kept moving — he sees a link, shared by someone in a community he half-follows, to a project he's never heard of, solving a problem adjacent to one he's been circling without quite naming for weeks.

He opens it.

## 55. What Pulls You Back

If you asked him to explain, cleanly, why he keeps doing this — why the badge coming off has never once, in seven-plus years, actually meant the thinking stops — he wouldn't reach for any of the answers that tend to circulate in the specific corner of the internet he has spent so much time inside. It isn't ambition, not in the naked, propulsive sense that word usually implies; ambition would have made him post more, market the TKI agent, turn the RAG evaluation dashboard into a product instead of a private tool nobody but him has ever opened. It isn't discipline either — discipline is a word for people who do the hard thing regardless of whether they want to, and most of what fills these hours, if he's honest, is exactly the opposite: things he wanted to do badly enough that no discipline was required to start them, only enough to occasionally stop.

What actually pulls him back, most nights, is something closer to an unfinished sentence. A tab left open past paragraph four. A reward curve that hasn't peaked yet. A number that doesn't quite reconcile and is going to keep not reconciling, quietly, in the back of his mind, until he goes and checks. It isn't a grand hunger for achievement. It's a much smaller, more specific discomfort — the same one that made him circle back to a discount-factor bug instead of accepting a flat reward curve, the same one that wouldn't let a chunking strategy stay wrong for longer than a beautifully overbuilt dashboard could distract him from it. Curiosity, if that word hasn't been worn too smooth by overuse to still mean anything specific. Not the polite, brochure version of curiosity. The version that looks, some nights, uncomfortably close to just being unable to leave a question alone.

He doesn't think this is a virtue, exactly, and he resists the frame that would turn it into one — the frame where staying up late chasing an idea is secretly the noble thing, the hustle culture just wearing a more literary coat. Plenty of the questions he's chased were trivial. Plenty of the nights produced nothing. The board-game repository from months ago solved a problem four people on Earth have, and he still thinks about the eleven lines of code at the bottom of it more than he thinks about most of what he's built for actual money. There's no consistent ledger here where the important questions get chased and the unimportant ones get left alone. He chases what itches. Some of what itches turns out to matter. Most of it doesn't, and he's stopped needing it to, because the chasing was never really conditional on the outcome in the first place.

What he can say, plainly, without dressing it up: nobody has ever paid him to read a four-star repository at ten p.m. Nobody ever will. He does it anyway, has done it more nights than he could count if he tried, and will very likely do it again tonight, on whatever the next tab turns out to hold — not because he's decided it's meaningful, but because meaningful was never really the question he was asking. The question, every time, all the way back to a spreadsheet full of test cases at Kalaage Creations, has only ever been the same small one. Is this actually correct. And underneath that one, quieter, rarely spoken out loud even to himself: what happens if I try this.

## 56. 3:00 AM

It's later than he meant it to be, again — the particular, familiar lateness that he has tried to describe from enough angles that repeating the description now would be redundant. The clock, if he looked at it, which he eventually does, says three.

There's a version of tonight that resembles the ChatGPT clone's first working stream, or the ring drawing itself for the sixth time, or the reward curve finally climbing instead of flatlining — a small, private, unwitnessed thing working the way it's supposed to, for an audience of exactly one, at an hour when the audience of one is the only audience that was ever actually going to be here. He won't describe which one it is tonight. It doesn't matter which one it is. That's rather the point he has been circling since the first tab opened in the first chapter: the specific project changes, month to month, obsession to obsession, and the shape underneath it doesn't.

He knows he should sleep. He has known this at three a.m. more times than he could count, and the knowing has never once, on its own, been sufficient to close the laptop — not because he lacks the willpower, exactly, but because there is, sitting open in another tab, a repository he hasn't finished reading, four stars, a stranger's decision from some other timezone about how to solve a problem nobody asked them to solve, and it is doing the thing it always does, the thing this account started with and never really stopped being about: making him wonder, entirely without meaning to, what would happen if he tried it too.

There is no lesson to attach to this. He has looked, across every part of this account, for the moment where the whole thing resolves into something he could say plainly at the end of a chapter, and it keeps refusing to arrive, and he's stopped expecting it to. The reconciliation report from the day job will still need checking tomorrow. The M.Tech assignment will still be due. The TKI agent will still be missing whatever the next tool turns out to be. None of that gets solved tonight, and none of it needed to be.

The clock says three. There's one more thing worth figuring out before it's worth admitting the night is actually over.

He opens the tab.
