## The Ring, Revisited

Long after the ScrollTrigger bug got fixed, long after the ring started drawing itself correctly on every page load instead of just the sixth refresh, he goes back into the data file one more time — not to fix anything, everything about it already works exactly as intended, but because a stray thought, the kind that arrives unprompted at an hour like this one, makes him want to actually check the math behind the number he picked by feel over a year ago.

Seven-plus years, the ring claims, against a ceiling he invented and never wrote down anywhere except as a single decimal buried in a TypeScript object: 0.72. He does the arithmetic properly this time, out of pure curiosity rather than any intention to change the value. If the implied ceiling is meant to represent something like a full, conventional career — say, twenty-five years from a reasonable starting point to a reasonable retirement — then seven-plus years lands closer to twenty-eight or thirty percent, nowhere near seventy-two. The number he chose by feel, it turns out, was never actually measuring years against a career-length ceiling at all. It was measuring something closer to confidence, or momentum, or how far along a road he privately felt he'd traveled, wearing the costume of a precise statistic because a precise statistic looks better on a hero section than an honest label like "vibes, updated occasionally" ever would.

He finds this less troubling than he expects to, turning it over. Every number on the site does this, to some degree — the skill-mix donut, the capability-growth chart's projected curves reaching all the way out to a year that hasn't happened yet, all of it built from a mixture of real measurement and confident invention, blended together so smoothly that a visitor, and increasingly he himself, can no longer easily tell where one ends and the other begins. This isn't unique to a personal portfolio. It's just more visible here, because he's the only author, the only editor, the only person who'll ever know exactly which numbers came from a stopwatch and which came from a feeling that needed a decimal point to look credible.

He doesn't change the ring. Seventy-two percent, wrong as a literal measurement, has become correct in a different way — accurate now to how long it's actually been sitting there, unquestioned, drawing itself in on every page load, a number he no longer entirely believes and has stopped needing to. Some things on a personal site, he's decided, get to stay exactly as arbitrary as the night they were chosen, provided you're honest with yourself, at least once, about what they actually are.

## No Ending, Just a Later Chapter

If he has a shape at all — and he's genuinely unsure it does, rereading the account of these months back to himself, unsure whether a shape got imposed after the fact or was actually there all along, hiding underneath nights that didn't feel like they were building toward anything while they were happening — the shape isn't a rise. It isn't a fall either. It's closer to the reward curve from the reinforcement learning assignment, months back now: jagged, noisy, trending somewhere on average that only becomes visible if you squint at enough of it at once and refuse to read too much into any single point along the way.

Some threads in this account resolved. The RL agent learned to win more than it lost. The reconciliation gate at work now runs on every pull request instead of existing only as a lesson learned the hard way. The TKI agent's repository has more than one contributor now, however modestly. Some threads didn't resolve and were never going to, not on the timeline a book needs to have an ending: the M.Tech continues, coursework by coursework, no closer to feeling finished than it did in an early chapter, because a degree pursued alongside a full-time job doesn't really have a dramatic finish line, just a slow accumulation of completed modules that will, eventually, add up to a credential nobody at his current job is likely to ask him to justify.

He's suspicious, closing this account rather than any grander project, of any version of it that would try to tie all these threads into a single lesson — the kind of closing paragraph that shows up in exactly the genre of writing he has tried hardest not to become, the one where the narrator has finally figured it all out, where the nights of scattered, unglamorous, half-successful curiosity resolve into a tidy sentence about growth or purpose or hard-won wisdom. Nothing about the last several months has resolved that cleanly, and he doesn't trust an account that claims otherwise, including, especially, his own.

What's true, plainly, without the lesson wrapped around it: the tabs keep opening. The reconciliation instinct that started in a spreadsheet at Kalaage Creations is still, seven-plus years and one uncomfortable amount of vocabulary later, the same question asked of new subjects. Some nights produce something. Most nights produce a slightly better understanding of something that will matter later, or won't, there's no way to know in advance which. He is not meaningfully closer to being finished with any of it than he was on the Tuesday this whole account opened with, and he's stopped expecting to be, because finished was never actually the shape this was ever going to take.

There will be another Tuesday. There always is. Whatever gets built on it hasn't happened yet, which is, if he's honest, the only part of any of this that's ever really been the point.
